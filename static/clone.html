Why recordings sound strange, and how to fix it
  • It's not just you. Everyone hates hearing their own recorded voice, because normally you also hear yourself through your skull. The recording is what others hear.
  • Processing is switched off here. Browsers and call apps run noise suppression, echo cancelling and auto-gain, which make voices sound watery and robotic. This page records the raw mic.
  • A hand-span away. Keep the mic 15-20 cm from your mouth, slightly off to the side so P and B sounds don't pop.
  • Soft room. Bare walls make echo, which gets cloned. A bedroom with curtains, a wardrobe full of clothes, or a duvet over your head works wonders. Turn off fans and the TV.
  • Same mic, same spot for every emotion, so the profile sounds like one person. A phone voice memo app often beats a laptop mic.
  • Act it, don't read it. Read the line twice silently first, then perform it as if it's real. Stand up for energy; smile for happy lines.
  • 8-12 seconds, one emotion. Leave half a second of silence at the start and end. If you stumble, just retake.
  • Loud emotions: step back for shouting so it doesn't distort; lean in a little for whispers.
